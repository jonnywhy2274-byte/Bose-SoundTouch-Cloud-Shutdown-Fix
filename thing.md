I think you might have only skimmed the first paragraph of that post, because almost everything you just described about how Controller Pro works is actually backwards:

1. **Standalone Mode doesn't redirect your speaker to an external server:** When you use the standalone app, it does **not** change the server IP on the speaker, open any external ports, or route your audio through someone else's server. The app talks 100% locally over your home Wi-Fi directly to the stock speaker, and the speaker pulls the radio stream straight from the broadcaster's URL via your router. Zero data goes to any middleman server.

2. **They *did* reverse-engineer the full Bose Marge & BMX servers (for both 100% Local LAN and Cloud):** If you read the second half of the post, their **Controller Cube** (which runs 100% locally on your own Wi-Fi via a Raspberry Pi, Home Assistant, or their plug-and-play box with zero subscriptions and zero internet exposure) emulates the entire Bose Marge/BMX cluster—restoring the 6 physical preset buttons with your phone off, custom OLED screen logos, Left/Right stereo pairing, and SMB/DLNA NAS drives.

3. **Why stuffing a server onto the speaker's internal chip is a dead end long-term:** The Bose SoundTouch runs on a 12-year-old single-core ARM chip with tiny RAM, fragile onboard NAND flash (`/mnt/nv`), and an ancient OpenSSL 1.0.x library frozen in firmware 27.0.6. A script piggybacking inside the speaker has two fatal flaws going forward:
   * **It can't transcode modern streams or TLS 1.3:** Major broadcasters (BBC, Global/LBC/Heart, US/EU networks) are rapidly shutting down legacy MP3 streams in favour of `.m3u8` HLS streams and modern HTTPS certificates that the speaker's old Linux kernel physically cannot decode. An external local node (like the Cube) or bridge can run live `ffmpeg` HLS-to-MP3 transcoding and SSL stripping on the fly; the speaker's own processor cannot.
   * **NAND Flash Wear & Multi-Room Sync:** Writing state files directly to the speaker's internal `/mnt/nv` flash risks wearing out irreplaceable onboard memory, and if you have multiple speakers, a loopback hack can't act as a central household server.

4. **Hobby Project vs. Long-Term Sustainability:** Free GitHub hobby projects are great while a volunteer dev is interested for a few weekends, but the moment broadcasters change their APIs or the developer moves on to another hobby, the repo gets abandoned and everyone is stuck again—juggling raw IP addresses in a mobile web browser. Paying £3.49 (less than the price of a coffee) for a dedicated, actively maintained native iOS/Android app is what actually keeps a project sustainable long-term.
